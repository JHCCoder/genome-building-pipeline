# Figure 7 segdup results paragraph — revised around "Cellular Response to Type II Interferon"

Drafted 2026-08-19. This version (a) uses the segmental-duplication background for
GO enrichment (foreground = 469 hotspot genes vs background = 6,814 segdup-overlapping
genes), and (b) focuses the gene-expression examination on the top surviving term,
"Cellular Response to Type II Interferon" (GO:0071346), instead of the Cajal-body term.

=============================================================================
REVISED PARAGRAPH
=============================================================================

We define the segdup pervasive region as the minimum number of segdup regions
that make up 80% of the total segdup lengths (Figure 7A). Using this criterion,
we identified the 1,426 longest segdup regions containing 417.9 Mbp of sequence,
which includes 1,271 gene features equating to 469 unique, non-LOC, genes
(Figure 7A). GO enrichment analysis of the 469 genes against a background of
segmentally duplicated genes revealed significant enrichment for type II
interferon signaling and host defense (cellular response to type II interferon:
8/17 genes, padj = 0.013; GO:0071346; response to type II interferon: 8/20 genes,
padj = 0.016; GO:0034341; defense response to virus: 16/74 genes, padj = 0.016;
GO:0051607; defense response to bacterium: 14/58 genes, padj = 0.016; GO:0042742),
inflammation (regulation of interleukin-1 beta production: 8/24 genes,
padj = 0.028; GO:0032651), and metabolism (primary alcohol metabolic process:
8/21 genes, padj = 0.017; GO:0034308; retinoid metabolic process: 8/24 genes,
padj = 0.028; GO:0001523). We wondered whether the gene features enriched in the
GO terms are transcriptionally active and decided to examine the genes enriched
in the term Cellular Response to Type II Interferon. Specifically, this pathway
comprised eight genes — GBP1, GBP5, GBP6, IFNG, CASP1, CCL3, LGALS9, and
SYNCRIP — which expand to 20 gene features (17 paralogs and 3 single-copy genes). The
guanylate-binding protein (GBP) family dominates this set, with GBP5 and GBP6
expanded into five and four paralogous copies, respectively (10 of the 20
features). The majority of these genes are transferred from the OctDeg1.0
annotation via Liftoff (e.g., SYNCRIP, IFNG, GBP5, GBP6, LGALS9, CASP1, CCL3),
while the remaining copies were added by the de novo braker annotation
(GBP5-DL1, GBP5-DL2, GBP5-DL3, IFNG-DL1, and GBP1) and could represent functional
paralogs or processed pseudogenes. We then visualized the associated segdup
links and positions of these genes and their paralogous features (Figure 7D).
We mapped degu transcriptomic data from 29 tissues across five organs
(NCBI BioProject PRJNA788430) to determine whether the 20 gene features are
transcriptionally active. The results show detectable expression of at least one
feature for each of the eight genes examined (Figure 7F). These data indicate
that segmentally duplicated genes in the degu genome are enriched for
functionally active genes involved in type II interferon signaling and host
defense.
