# GO enrichment with the segdup-overlapping background

## Setup
- Foreground: 469 unique non-LOC genes in the 80% segdup "pervasive" hotspot.
- Background: 6,814 unique non-LOC genes overlapping ANY segdup region
  (i.e., hotspot genes compared against all other segdup-overlapping genes,
  instead of the whole-genome background of 17,235 genes).
- Library: human_GO_bp_2025.gmt (Enrichr GO Biological Process 2025).
- Method: gseapy.enrichr, custom local GMT + background; adjusted P = BH.

## Interpretation

- The Cajal body enrichment -- the centerpiece of the current paragraph
  (Cct3/Cct4/Cct7/Larp7) -- disappears under the segdup background
  (padj 0.017 -> 0.18). This is expected: those four genes are themselves
  segmentally duplicated, so they are already over-represented across ALL
  segdup regions. The genome-wide test was really detecting "segdup genes are
  enriched for Cajal body factors", not "the pervasive hotspot is specifically
  enriched for them".

- What survives the more stringent test is a coherent immune / host-defense
  signature: type-II interferon response (GBP family, CASP1, IFNG, LGALS9,
  SYNCRIP), defense to virus (IFIT/IFITM/TRIM/RNASE/IFNL/SAMHD1/MX2), defense
  to bacterium (LYZ, MPEG1, S100A8, RNASE, FPR2, SFTPD), NK-cell regulation,
  and IL-1 beta production. These form a biologically coherent module.

## Recommendation

Worthwhile to redo, with two decisions:

1. Which question the paper is actually asking:
   - "Are segmentally duplicated genes enriched for X (vs the genome)?" ->
     foreground should be ALL segdup-overlapping genes (6,814), background =
     genome. This is the right test if the claim is about segdups in general.
   - "Is the pervasive (largest 80%) hotspot specifically enriched for X (vs
     other segdups)?" -> use the segdup background, as computed here. This is
     the right test if the claim is specifically about the pervasive region.

2. The surviving signal is the more compelling story: the pervasive hotspot is
   specifically enriched for immune / interferon / host-defense genes, not for
   Cajal body factors. If reframed this way, the paragraph's conclusion should
   shift from "Cajal body function" to "immune and host-defense function".

Caveats:
- Only 9 terms survive; the signal is sparser than the genome-wide test.
- The 4/5 terms (NK-cell cytotoxicity, symbiont suppression) rest on only four
  genes and should be treated cautiously.
- The Cajal body result is not "wrong" -- it is real for segdup genes
  collectively -- it just is not specific to the pervasive hotspot.
